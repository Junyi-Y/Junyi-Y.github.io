Reading note

Palaces for the People

Eric Klinenberg · reading note

The argument. Social infrastructure, the libraries, cafes, and barbershops where people linger regardless of consumption, is the material foundation of social life. Isolation is a health threat on the order of obesity and smoking, and these third places mitigate it. In the 1995 Chicago heat wave, a neighborhood with intact social infrastructure (Auburn Gresham) checked on its vulnerable neighbors and survived far better than a depopulated one (Englewood). When the spaces disappear, through deindustrialization, redevelopment, or smart machines replacing corner stores, ties collapse and people die alone.

Where my work goes further. Klinenberg shows third places provide social support, largely through narrative and extreme events. I treat them as decentralized care environments and ask the measurable question he leaves open: what are the everyday health consequences of their disappearance, in depression, hypertension control, and emergency-room visits, and how can non-medical spaces redistribute load off centralized hospitals in aging cities.

Source · Palaces for the People (Crown, 2018) · Eric Klinenberg
