Reading note

What is a healthy place?

Ann Forsyth (2019) · reading note

The argument. There is no single healthy place, but a range of overlapping models: healthy built environments, collaborative healthy cities, age-friendly communities, child-friendly communities, healthcare industrial cities, and smart health environments. Forsyth organizes them along six dimensions, and stresses that the built environment shapes health indirectly, through behavior and social conditions, not on its own.

The gap it names. Across the typology, healthcare appears mainly as industrial development or monitoring technology, not as the small distributed nodes people use day to day. Neighborhood healthcare infrastructure is rarely treated as a spatial system inside everyday life.

For my research. This is the exact opening. My work takes the neighborhood healthcare infrastructure the typology leaves out and studies it as a spatial system, pairing spatial analysis with behavior rather than reading form alone.

Source · “What is a Healthy Place? Models for Cities and Neighborhoods” (2019) · annforsyth.net
