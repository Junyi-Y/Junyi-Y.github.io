Healthcare programming and circulation, where the question began.

Christner Architects, St. Louis · 2024 · project team, MU Children's Hospital surgery suite renovation.

On this 26,000-square-foot children's surgery suite at Christner Architects, I worked with the team on how a healthcare floor organizes movement for patients, families, and staff. It is also where my research question began.

The problem

A surgery floor is built for throughput. The plan is organized around operating and imaging suites, and corridor widths are set by how fast a bed must move. The question that kept surfacing was how much of the floor can go to rest and social use rather than to movement, and who loses when it cannot.

The design

I worked through the floor's programming and circulation, from the plan and the care-team desk in detail to the waiting, play, and recovery spaces where families actually spend their time.

The moment that started the research

I proposed placing seating along a south-facing corridor, to give families a place to rest. It was rejected, because the beds needed to pass through quickly. The logic was sound, and it made a trade-off visible: in large hospitals, efficiency leaves little room for social and restorative space.

In my research

That trade-off, at the building scale, is the one my dissertation follows out to the neighborhood: whether the space around care leaves room for the people who use it.
